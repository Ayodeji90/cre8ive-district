# Cre8ive District — Buyer Interview Guide and Offer Tests

**Purpose:** Obtain customer voice before finalizing the first buyer focus, package, and permanent pricing.  
**Status:** Ready for founder-led interviews; no interviews have yet been conducted.  
**Recommended sample:** Five recent buyers/active evaluators: three Nigeria-based, two international or diaspora-led. Treat results as directional qualitative evidence, not a representative survey.

## 1. Who to interview

Recruit people who personally chose, paid for, or actively evaluated branding, brand strategy, website, or content/visibility help within the last 24 months.

| Count | Suggested participant profile | What this tests |
|---:|---|---|
| 2 | Nigeria-based founders who launched a business and bought/considered branding | Launch trigger, starting scope, budget, and proof needs |
| 1 | Nigeria-based owner of an established service/small business who refreshed a brand or website | Inconsistency, rebrand vs refresh, digital credibility, and continuation needs |
| 1 | International or diaspora-led business that hired or considered a remote studio | Trust, time zones, payment, contracts, portfolio, and remote collaboration |
| 1 | Recent evaluator who postponed or declined a branding/web project | Price, timing, trust, alternatives, internal capability, and reasons to delay |

Avoid recruiting only close friends, existing design clients, or people who know the intended offer. If possible, include prospects who selected another provider and ask what drove that decision. Do not describe a respondent's comments as representative of a country or market.

## 2. Recruitment and consent

The founder should make introductions or send invitations personally; no one should be contacted under a fabricated buyer identity. Explain that this is 30–45 minutes of research for Cre8ive District, not a sales call. Ask permission before recording or quoting. Offer anonymity; do not collect confidential customer data or request private proposals. A thank-you gesture is optional and should not depend on positive feedback.

**Invitation text the founder can adapt:**

> I’m researching how founders choose branding, website, and visibility support so I can shape a useful offer for Cre8ive District. Could I ask you about the last time you went through that decision? It would take about 30–45 minutes. This is research rather than a sales pitch; I can keep your answers anonymous, and I’ll ask separately before recording or quoting anything.

## 3. 40-minute interview script

### Opening (2 minutes)

“Thank you. I’m interested in what actually happened the last time you needed brand or website help. There are no right answers. I’m testing my assumptions, so please be candid, including if you chose not to hire anyone. I won’t use your name or record unless you agree.”

### The real event (8 minutes)

1. Tell me about the last time you decided the brand, website, or online presence needed attention.
2. What happened that made it important then? What was going on in the business?
3. What were you worried would happen if you did nothing?
4. What did you need to be different when the work was done?
5. Who else was involved in the decision, and who approved the spend?

**Probe for concrete events:** launch date, new product, customer feedback, inconsistent materials, lost proposal, low trust, expansion, new audience, or founder time burden. Do not offer examples unless the interviewee gets stuck.

### Search and alternatives (8 minutes)

6. What was the first thing you did to solve it?
7. Where did you look for help, and what did you type/ask/search? Did you ask anyone for referrals?
8. Which people, studios, freelancers, tools, or internal options did you compare?
9. What made a provider feel credible or risky? What proof did you inspect?
10. Did you consider doing it yourself or using templates/AI? Why did you choose or reject that?

Ask if they are comfortable showing a public site, a proposal with sensitive details hidden, or an example of the work they selected. Never request passwords, private analytics, or confidential client files.

### Evaluation and decision (8 minutes)

11. What criteria mattered most when you compared options?
12. What did you understand about each provider's deliverables, timing, revisions, and handoff before paying?
13. What was confusing or missing?
14. What did the project cost or what range were you prepared to spend? What was included/excluded?
15. What nearly made you say no? What finally made you say yes—or decide to wait?
16. If you did not proceed, what would need to change before you revisit it?

Do not ask “Would you pay ₦800,000 for this?” until the behavioral questions are complete. Avoid treating polite enthusiasm as purchase intent.

### Delivery experience and outcome (6 minutes)

17. What went well or poorly after work began?
18. How were strategy, design, website/content, and marketing connected—or separated—across providers?
19. How did feedback, revisions, communication, time zones, and payment work?
20. What did you receive at handoff, and could your team use it without the provider?
21. What changed for the business? Which changes can you actually observe or measure?
22. What help did you still need afterward?

### Offer concept test (last 6 minutes)

Only after learning the participant's story, show this concise concept:

> **Cre8ive District Brand Foundation (pilot concept):** four weeks; discovery and current brand audit; focused buyer/competitor research; positioning and messaging; a visual identity system with a short usage guide and three social templates; a 30-day visibility plan with eight post prompts. Website build, photography/video, printing, paid advertising, posting/community management, naming clearance, and guaranteed results are separate/out of scope. Proposed pilot price: ₦800,000 for the first three suitable projects.

Then ask:

23. Which part is most/least useful for the situation you described?
24. What is missing or unnecessary? Would you expect a website or actual content production in this scope?
25. What alternative would you compare this against?
26. Does the four-week sequence fit a real business trigger you have? What would delay it?
27. Only now: how does ₦800,000 compare with what you expected and with your past quote/spend? What would make it feel justified or not?
28. What would you need to see from the studio before trusting it with this work?

Price response is not a commitment. Record exact reasoning, past spend, and actual next step separately.

### Close (2 minutes)

29. What have I not asked that mattered in your decision?
30. Is there one other person who recently made this choice and might be willing to talk? Ask for an introduction only with their consent.

## 5. How to synthesize five interviews

After five conversations, create a simple evidence table with rows for each buyer situation and columns for trigger, urgency, discovery source, alternatives, criteria, price context, objections, proof need, and next purchase step.

Use these decision rules:

- Keep a buyer segment in the launch wedge if at least three of five interviews describe a recognizable, urgent problem the team can serve; still treat this as directional evidence.
- If participants repeatedly expected a website, content production, or paid management inside a brand package, make the boundary visible and test a staged/add-on offer; do not silently expand scope.
- If actual comparable quotes are consistently far below ₦800,000, inspect scope and buyer fit before lowering the price. If comparable budgets and expected value are higher, test a higher-price tier. Do not average unlike packages.
- If referrals dominate actual discovery, allocate founder time to partner/referral work. If prospects show evidence of finding case studies through Behance or search, invest in detailed case publication and indexing. Do not infer channel value from likes.
- If remote trust, payment, or legal concerns repeatedly block a sale, resolve operational readiness before promising international service broadly.

Five interviews answer what to investigate next; they do not establish statistical demand or prove a price. Follow with qualified enquiry, proposal, win/loss, delivery-time, and margin data from real projects.

## 6. Deep competitor research that still needs founder-enabled access

The desk review now covers detailed public offers, pricing signals, process, proof assets, and enquiry calls to action for four close peers. These activities remain incomplete because their data are not available through the current research interface and require lawful, non-deceptive access:

- A dated review of each competitor's latest 30 Instagram/TikTok posts, coded by format, topic, proof, CTA, public views/likes/comments where visible, and posting cadence.
- A location-specific Google results baseline for “branding agency Lagos,” “brand identity Nigeria,” and selected international equivalents, using a controlled browser or SEO rank tool. Search result order varies by location, language, personalization, and time.
- Five or more buyer interviews, including at least one buyer who chose a competitor and one who did not buy.
- Mystery shopping only if the founder explicitly approves a truthful, non-wasteful inquiry protocol. Do not invent a fake company/project or consume competitor sales time without permission.
