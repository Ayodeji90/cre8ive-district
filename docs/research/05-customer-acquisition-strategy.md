# Cre8ive District — Customer Acquisition Strategy

**Status:** Launch strategy for founder review; test against actual enquiry and sales data  
**Prepared:** 29 October 2026  
**Market:** Nigeria and international clients  
**Purpose:** Establish a lean acquisition system that brings suitable businesses into a clear, credible conversation about brand strategy, identity, visibility, websites, and related creative work.

## 1. Executive recommendation

Do not launch with a broad campaign aimed at “any brand.” Keep the service area open to Nigeria and international clients, but test a small number of concrete buyer situations and a clear lead offer. The first 90 days should prioritize **warm relationships and referrals**, supported by a conversion-ready website, consistent proof-led social content, and a small number of tightly scoped partnerships. Add search, marketplaces, outbound, and paid promotion according to observed lead quality and operating capacity.

For a new studio, the first acquisition problem is often proof and trust. Use the founder's access, a clear process, honest labelled concept work, permissioned case studies, and a simple enquiry path. Do not invent client outcomes, client names, scarcity, or campaign results.

**Recommended initial audience hypotheses:**

1. Founders preparing to launch who need strategic clarity, identity, and useful launch assets.
2. Growing businesses whose brand is inconsistent or no longer reflects the business, and that need an identity or web/creative refresh.
3. International prospects who can work remotely with a Nigeria-based studio and can be served within the studio's current payment, communication, and delivery capabilities.

The first two are customer problems; the third is a delivery/geography hypothesis. Research should find which segment and acquisition source produce qualified conversations and projects with workable margins.

## 2. Evidence and limitations

- PwC's 2024 Nigeria MSME survey reports more than 500 CEOs across 13 sectors and 29 states, primarily through an online survey. In the technology section, the report lists WhatsApp Business (23%), Facebook (19%), Instagram (15%), email marketing (10%), and Twitter/X (9%) among technologies used by respondents. This is evidence that those tools are present in the surveyed MSME mix, not a ranking of the best channels to acquire branding clients; the report's respondent composition and question wording matter. [PwC MSME Survey 2024](https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/assets/pdf/2024-pwc-msme-survey-report.pdf).
- PwC also reports that 54% of surveyed MSME owners rated social media/digitization very relevant and 16% relevant to their business strategy. This supports testing digital education and service discovery, but does not demonstrate willingness to buy branding or a conversion rate.
- A 2026 LinkedIn/Ipsos small-business research preview reports that referrals and founder-led outreach are important in its sample. The stated sample is 300 U.S. small-business decision-makers at firms with 2–50 employees, so its percentages must not be presented as Nigerian or universal market facts. Use the finding to motivate a referral experiment, then measure Cre8ive District's own results. [LinkedIn x Ipsos 2026 preview](https://business.linkedin.com/small-business/resources/2026-small-business-study).
- Google Search Essentials recommends helpful, reliable, people-first content and warns that meeting technical guidelines does not guarantee crawling, indexing, or ranking. SEO is a long-term discoverability asset, not a guaranteed short-term lead source. [Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials).
- Upwork Project Catalog lets buyers compare projects by scope, price, terms, timing, reviews, and samples. A marketplace therefore rewards clear deliverables and proof, while putting the studio alongside highly comparable listings. Test one platform only after the offer is scopeable. [Upwork Project Catalog](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/10408677826963-What-is-Project-Catalog-on-Upwork).
- Nigeria's NDPC FAQ says the NDPA applies to organizations operating in Nigeria and explains personal data processing broadly. A 2026 legal guide summarizes the 2025 GAID as requiring consent for direct marketing, plus a right to object. Direct outreach rules can depend on the channel, data, and recipient jurisdiction; get qualified advice before running campaigns or uploading prospect/contact lists to marketing systems. [NDPC FAQ](https://ndpc.gov.ng/faqs/); [ICLG Nigeria Data Protection 2026](https://iclg.com/practice-areas/data-protection-laws-and-regulations/nigeria/).

**Research quality note:** Public survey data is directional. Competitor pages show what agencies claim to offer, not the size of demand or customer preference. The strongest next evidence is direct interviews with the actual prospects and referral partners Cre8ive District wants to serve.

## 3. Offer and conversion path

### The lead offer

Lead with a founder-approved **Brand Foundation** engagement that solves a specific buyer problem: clarify the business's position and story, create a distinctive identity system, and prepare agreed assets the client can put to work. Define inclusions, exclusions, client inputs, revision rounds, timing, fee, payment schedule, IP/handoff, and change requests before publishing a fixed package or price.

Use a short fit call as the low-friction first action. It should establish the problem, readiness, decision-maker, timing, expected scope, budget fit, and delivery capacity; it should not become unpaid speculative strategy work. Offer tailored scopes only after discovery.

### Funnel

| Stage | Prospect question | Acquisition asset / action | Studio response | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | “Why should I pay attention?” | Referral, useful content, partner introduction, search result, community contribution | State who the studio helps and the problem it addresses | Qualified visits / conversations by source |
| Consideration | “Can you understand my situation and do this well?” | Website service/process pages, concept work or case study, founder explanation, social profile | Show process, scope boundaries, proof, and how work is delivered remotely | Service-page engagement, replies, proof questions |
| Enquiry | “What should I do next?” | Project form, email, WhatsApp, or fit-call link | Acknowledge, log, and qualify with a human owner | Enquiries, time to first response, completion/drop-off |
| Decision | “What will I get, when, and at what cost?” | Discovery and written proposal | Set deliverables, exclusions, schedule, fees, milestones, responsibilities, and expiry | Qualified calls, proposals, acceptance, sales cycle |
| Delivery / referral | “Did the work solve the agreed problem?” | Approval gates, final handoff, feedback request | Deliver, measure client experience, request testimonial/work permission separately | Margin, revision rate, satisfaction, repeat/referral lead |

The website should make the WhatsApp/email handoff accurate and explain whether the form stores anything. If a CRM or booking flow is added, disclose the relevant data handling and use a tool that the studio can operate.

## 5. Proof and trust assets

Build these in order; do not wait for a large portfolio before launching, but be explicit about what is a concept:

1. **Three useful studio concepts** for plausible customer situations. Each should include a short fictional/brief context clearly identified as self-initiated, the strategic question, design decisions, and applications across touchpoints. Do not imply a real client or measured result.
2. **Founder/studio introduction** that explains why the studio exists, its working style, and who leads projects. State true experience only.
3. **Process example** showing discovery, direction, identity, application, review, and handoff in a transparent way.
4. **A strong website case** demonstrating mobile craft, page hierarchy, content clarity, accessibility, and a credible conversion path. The studio website itself can demonstrate engineering and design quality, but should not be presented as client proof.
5. **Client proof as earned:** ask for permission to publish a case, name, logo, quote, or result; preserve the approved wording and attribution.

Useful proof beats unsupported scale claims. Record evidence as a source and approval: who approved it, which channels it may appear on, and whether any sensitive information must be removed.

## 6. Qualification and lead handling

### Qualification questions

- What is the business, product/service, and customer?
- Is this a new launch, a refresh, a visibility problem, or a specific website/campaign need?
- What prompted the project now? What happens if it is delayed?
- Who decides and who will give feedback?
- Which deliverables are needed, and what is explicitly out of scope?
- What timing and investment range are realistic?
- Can the studio deliver the work with current skills, partners, and capacity?
- For an international prospect: what time zones, working language, payment route, contract/invoice needs, and decision process apply?

### Lead tracking fields

Maintain one restricted-access tracker with: enquiry date; source/campaign/referrer; country/time zone; contact/business; service and customer situation; consent/source of personal contact data where applicable; urgency/timing; budget fit; decision-maker; current stage; next action and due date; proposal amount and status; won/lost/unqualified reason; expected delivery hours; actual collected amount; and permission status for any public proof.

### Response routine

Set a realistic first-response expectation based on actual coverage. Acknowledge receipt, explain the next step, and avoid promising a response time the studio cannot meet. Where the enquiry began in WhatsApp or email, reply in that channel unless the prospect agrees otherwise. Review every open lead at least weekly and close the loop on proposals by the agreed follow-up date.

## 7. 30/60/90-day experiments

Treat activity counts below as starter planning targets, not performance guarantees. Adjust after seeing available hours and lead quality.

### Days 1–30: establish signal

- Complete the service offer, proof examples, website conversion path, profiles, lead tracker, and response owner.
- Hold **8–12 structured conversations** with prospective buyers/referral partners across launch-stage and growing-business situations. Ask about actual buying triggers, objections, alternatives, trust needs, geography, and investment language; do not pitch throughout the interview.
- Make **10 specific warm introduction requests** to people who can plausibly refer a suitable business. Ask permission before sharing contact details or adding people to a list.
- Publish a sustainable content test on two primary social channels; keep a small idea log of hooks, audience questions, replies, profile visits, and qualified actions.
- Log every enquiry by source and classify it as qualified, unqualified, or not yet known.

**Review:** Which buyer problem was easiest to explain? Which source produced a real conversation? What proof or scope detail was missing? What work can the team actually deliver?

### Days 31–60: improve the strongest path

- Choose one primary segment and one adjacent segment for the next test; update page and profile language to their real customer vocabulary.
- Turn one completed project or concept into a proof asset that shows the brief, reasoning, application, and result/status truthfully.
- Test **one partner path** (for example, a web developer, printer, photographer, adviser, or founder community) with a clear reciprocal referral process.
- Test one marketplace profile only if there are quality portfolio examples and a pre-scoped offer; cap proposal time and record qualified inquiry rate and effective hourly economics.
- Review response time, show rate for discovery calls, proposal turnaround, win/loss reasons, and delivery capacity.

### Days 61–90: scale only what is working

- Keep channels that produce qualified conversations and profitable projects; stop or reduce low-fit activity.
- Consider a small paid test only with a specific audience/offer hypothesis, a working contact path, consent-compliant data handling, a named response owner, and a hard spend cap the business can afford to lose.
- Compare paid and organic results by **cost per qualified conversation**, proposal, and won project—not just reach, clicks, or cheap leads.
- Review whether the studio can support international prospects operationally before adding country-specific claims or targeting: payment, contract, time-zone coverage, taxes/invoicing, IP terms, and support expectations.
- Decide whether to productize an offer, add a CRM, hire/contract capacity, or run another market test based on contribution margin and capacity.

## 8. Measurement dashboard

Track weekly, review monthly:

- **Attention:** relevant profile/site visits, search queries and impressions, content saves/shares/replies (context only).
- **Intent:** direct enquiries, fit-call requests, referral introductions, service-page-to-contact actions.
- **Qualification:** qualified enquiries by source and segment; response time; discovery calls booked/held; disqualification reasons.
- **Sales:** proposals sent; acceptance rate; average time from first conversation to decision; source of won work; reasons lost.
- **Economics:** contracted and collected revenue; delivery hours; contribution margin after direct costs; effective hourly return; payment delays.
- **Capacity/quality:** active projects versus available hours; on-time milestones; revision/change requests; client satisfaction; referrals/repeat engagements.

Set target numbers from a confirmed weekly capacity and first-month baseline. A small number of high-fit projects may be more valuable than a large volume of low-fit enquiries.

## 9. Outreach, data, and platform safeguards

- Obtain appropriate permission before adding an individual to a marketing list or sending direct marketing, and provide a simple way to stop future messages. Nigeria's NDPA/GAID and other client jurisdictions may apply; have a qualified adviser confirm exact rules, particularly for cross-border contact lists, cookies, analytics, and retargeting.
- Do not scrape or buy personal email/phone lists. Do not send unsolicited bulk WhatsApp messages. The 2026 Nigeria guide describes consent requirements for direct marketing under the GAID and says the rules can apply to personal data in B2B contexts; see legal source above.
- Keep project enquiries separate from marketing opt-in. A person asking for a proposal has not automatically agreed to receive a newsletter or unrelated offers.
- Store only lead information needed for qualification/follow-up, restrict access, define retention, and remove data when no longer needed under the approved policy.
- Respect platform/community rules, do not misrepresent outcomes, and do not post client work or testimonials without permission.

This section is operational guidance, not legal advice. Confirm current requirements in every target jurisdiction before launching direct-marketing, tracking, or paid-retargeting activity.

## 10. Founder decisions before execution

1. Which one or two buyer situations should be the first 90-day focus?
2. What is the first offer's confirmed scope, minimum viable price, capacity, and exclusions?
3. Which portfolio work and concepts can be shown, and who can approve public use?
4. Which time zones and countries can the studio reliably serve today?
5. Which inbox/WhatsApp account, response owner, working hours, and follow-up policy are approved?
6. What payment routes/currencies and contract/invoicing arrangements are available for international work?
7. Which platform profiles already exist, who can publish, and what weekly content time is sustainable?
8. Is there an approved budget for marketplaces, events, tools, or paid advertising? What spend ceiling is affordable to lose?
9. Who owns privacy policy, consent records, analytics decisions, and data deletion?
