# Cre8ive District — Competitor & Reference Scan

**Prepared:** 28 October 2026  
**Coverage:** Public-facing agency/studio websites, reviewed on the date above.  
**Purpose:** Learn how comparable studios position and present their work; identify choices Cre8ive District can test. This is a curated sample, not an exhaustive market map or a ranking.

## How to read this scan

Agency claims, service lists, project examples, and calls to action below are observations from each agency's own website. Experience totals, project totals, client relationships, and other self-reported claims have not been independently audited. “Cre8ive District can learn” and “risk of copying” are analysis, not statements made by the agencies. The international examples are references for practices and positioning, not direct local competitors on price or customer segment.

## Nigeria

### 1. Bantu & Co. — Lagos

- **Positioning observed:** A Lagos brand and design studio that grounds design in the people and places brands serve; the site uses locally specific cultural references and frames work as “people work.”
- **Services and proof observed:** Brand strategy, identity/design systems and related brand touchpoints; the site links services to selected work and studio information.
- **Lead path observed:** Project enquiry through a prominent “Start a project”/contact path; work and services are easy to browse.
- **Useful lesson:** Make local understanding visible through the way the studio thinks about customers and contexts, not just through “African” visual motifs.
- **Risk of copying:** Similar vernacular language and place references will feel derivative unless they come from Cre8ive District's own point of view and customer insight.
- **Source:** [Bantu & Co.](https://bantuandco.net/)

### 2. Dá Design Studio — Lagos

- **Positioning observed:** Boutique brand identity and art direction studio; its public work positions it around expressive visual systems and craft.
- **Services and proof observed:** Identity, motion, illustration, guidelines, print, packaging and logo work appear in project labels, including Green Africa Airways, Cowrywise, Danfo Std and Market March.
- **Lead path observed:** Direct email and phone contact, with work, studio, and contact navigation.
- **Useful lesson:** Show the parts and applications of an identity system, including motion and real-world touchpoints, rather than presenting a logo in isolation.
- **Risk of copying:** A portfolio-first, highly art-directed presentation only works if Cre8ive District has enough authentic work and consistent craft to support it.
- **Source:** [Dá Design Studio](https://dadesign.studio/)

### 3. Image & Time — Lagos

- **Positioning observed:** Creative/advertising agency that connects identity, strategy, campaigns, culture, film, and digital under the idea of work people feel.
- **Services and proof observed:** Case examples include Union Bank, Maggi, Haier Thermocool, Seplat, and Mr Bigg's. The work summaries explain a brand or audience challenge and the creative response.
- **Lead path observed:** Work, people, and “Start a project” are top-level routes.
- **Useful lesson:** Explain the problem behind the creative decision and how the work lives in culture; use specific case stories instead of a gallery of attractive assets alone.
- **Risk of copying:** Large campaign language and high-profile brand proof can set expectations that a young boutique studio cannot substantiate yet.
- **Source:** [Image & Time](https://www.imageandtime.com/)

### 4. 1904 Studios — Lagos

- **Positioning observed:** Design and technology studio focused on business transformation across brand, product, digital experience, and technology.
- **Services and proof observed:** Separate Brand, Product, Digital, and Technology capability areas; selected work is shown alongside a business-problem framing and project links.
- **Lead path observed:** “Book a call” and “Begin the conversation” provide direct routes to a sales conversation.
- **Useful lesson:** Explain why adjacent services belong together by tying them to a customer or business problem. This makes cross-selling more coherent than a long capability list.
- **Risk of copying:** Broad technology positioning implies deeper engineering capacity, team depth, and delivery governance; Cre8ive District should not make the same promise before confirming those capabilities.
- **Source:** [1904 Studios](https://www.1904studios.com/)

### 5. Adisa Designs — Lagos

- **Positioning observed:** Boutique studio focused on startups and consumer brands across West Africa, with a stated emphasis on culturally relevant design and regional understanding.
- **Services and proof observed:** Brand strategy/identity, product experiences, and visual systems; its site highlights a defined team, work, process, and contact routes. Team size and years are self-reported.
- **Lead path observed:** “Start a Project” and “Let's Talk,” alongside work and process navigation.
- **Useful lesson:** Narrow the audience and show who is involved. A founder can build trust through access to the people doing the work rather than implying a large agency.
- **Risk of copying:** “West Africa focus” is only differentiating if Cre8ive District can demonstrate relevant market insight and delivery beyond Lagos.
- **Source:** [Adisa Designs](https://adisadesigns.com/)

### 6. TMA Brand Consulting — Lagos

- **Positioning observed:** Design and strategy agency combining brand, digital experience, marketing, and creative execution.
- **Services and proof observed:** Broad menu spanning brand strategy, naming, identity, web, marketing, social, campaign development, market research, and go-to-market strategy. The site presents a six-stage process from discovery to optimization, selected work, and self-reported experience/project totals.
- **Lead path observed:** “Work With Us,” services, process, and contact routes; the homepage states a response expectation.
- **Useful lesson:** Show a clear engagement process and make it easy to move from services to a conversation.
- **Risk of copying:** A very broad menu can blur a new studio's specialism; numerical proof and response promises should not be published without evidence and operational capacity.
- **Source:** [TMA Brand Consulting](https://www.tmabrandconsulting.com/)

## International references

### 1. PORTO ROCHA — New York and London

- **Positioning observed:** Global strategy and design agency working across business, identity, products, and experiences.
- **Services and proof observed:** Strategy, verbal and visual identity, digital, motion, and 3D; a substantial project index includes case narratives for brands such as Tinder, Gemini, Nike, MoMA PS1, and Robinhood.
- **Lead path observed:** Project browsing is the main public proof path; the site describes the agency and its offering, with contact available through its global presence.
- **Useful lesson:** Connect brand strategy, identity, and digital behavior as one system, while showing project-specific outputs.
- **Risk of copying:** Global-client positioning, extensive disciplines, and highly produced work are backed by a large established practice and are not a template for a lean launch.
- **Source:** [PORTO ROCHA — About](https://www.portorocha.com/about)

### 2. Nucleus — London / international

- **Positioning observed:** Digital branding agency for founders and business leaders creating, repositioning, or rebranding businesses.
- **Services and proof observed:** Brand and digital strategy, naming, identity, websites, apps, and digital experiences; case studies are categorized and explain client briefs and solutions. Its homepage describes a proprietary AI brand agent, Third Lens®.
- **Lead path observed:** Strong service and case-study navigation leads into contact; “News & views” adds expertise content.
- **Useful lesson:** Organize proof by client need and show the brief, response, and outcome. Treat AI as part of a defined method or tool, not a vague adjective.
- **Risk of copying:** Proprietary AI product claims and decades of case work would require real capability and evidence; Cre8ive District should not suggest an equivalent system without building and validating one.
- **Source:** [Nucleus](https://www.nucleus.co.uk/)

### 3. Motto — United States

- **Positioning observed:** Strategic branding agency for companies in technology, culture, and innovation, aimed at founders and executive teams during growth or reinvention.
- **Services and proof observed:** Business strategy, brand positioning, category definition, design, and transformation; the site combines selected work with signature programs, a method, articles, workshops, and a 2026 Future of Brand report.
- **Lead path observed:** Visitors can explore structured programs, workshops, work, and “Work with us.”
- **Useful lesson:** Productize expertise into named programs and useful educational material, making the next step clearer than “we do everything.”
- **Risk of copying:** Executive-level strategy language and research reports need robust expertise and a specific audience; don't imitate authority signals without substance.
- **Source:** [Motto](https://wearemotto.com/)

### 4. Takt — Canada / North America

- **Positioning observed:** Brand and digital agency with explicit service lines for product design, brand strategy and identity, websites, campaigns, and branded content.
- **Services and proof observed:** Case studies appear alongside sector groupings and testimonials; insights are tied to specific audience or industry questions.
- **Lead path observed:** Clear contact path sits alongside services, industries, work, and insights.
- **Useful lesson:** Use the customer's industry or problem as a way to help them find relevant work; pair service pages with detailed case studies and practical insights.
- **Risk of copying:** Industry verticals only help when there is enough relevant expertise and work in each. Avoid making a menu of sectors based on aspiration alone.
- **Source:** [Takt](https://takt.com/)

### 5. Soochy — Porto, Portugal

- **Positioning observed:** Small independent agency presenting multidisciplinary creative work with both conceptual and functional aims.
- **Services and proof observed:** Branding, print, web, UX/UI, advertising, packaging, events, photography, editorial, and illustration; the portfolio shows project labels across branding, design, action, and digital.
- **Lead path observed:** Portfolio and studio information lead to a direct project enquiry.
- **Useful lesson:** A compact studio can communicate range through coherent project examples and recurring principles.
- **Risk of copying:** A very broad service list can make it hard for a new agency to be remembered for one thing; the portfolio has to prove the range.
- **Source:** [Soochy](https://soochy.com/)

### 6. Calacatta Concept — Porto, Portugal

- **Positioning observed:** Boutique branding, web design, and visual content studio working internationally.
- **Services and proof observed:** Brand strategy, logo and identity, guidelines, print, websites/landing pages, social media, SEO, photography, copywriting, and video content.
- **Lead path observed:** The site states availability for bespoke collaborations and has a direct “Contact us” route.
- **Useful lesson:** State availability and invite a specific kind of collaboration; connect brand work to the assets needed to launch it.
- **Risk of copying:** Availability messaging only works when capacity is actively maintained; don't promise a bespoke end-to-end content pipeline unless delivery partners and costs are clear.
- **Source:** [Calacatta Concept](https://www.calacattaconcept.pt/)

## Patterns across the sample

These are observations from the selected sites, not a statistical study of the whole market:

1. **The strongest sites frame a customer problem or point of view before listing capabilities.** Examples include a business outgrowing its systems (1904), design grounded in place and people (Bantu & Co.), and brands facing a moment of growth or reinvention (Motto).
2. **Proof is specific.** Project names, applied identity work, case summaries, and process are more informative than unqualified claims such as “creative solutions.” Several firms combine a work archive with explanation of the brief and result.
3. **The enquiry action is visible.** “Start a project,” “Book a call,” “Work with us,” and “Contact us” are common; the action is reinforced in service and case-study paths.
4. **Studios distinguish themselves through focus, not just service count.** Some specialize in identity systems (Dá), a geographic or audience lens (Adisa), broad strategy/creative execution (TMA), or brand-to-technology transformation (1904).
5. **Local context can be a strategic asset.** Nigerian examples make Lagos or West African context explicit. The opportunity is to show customer understanding and cultural fluency in the work, rather than add generic cultural decoration.

## Implications for Cre8ive District

### A practical position to test

Position Cre8ive District as a **Nigeria-based, hands-on brand partner for founders and growing businesses in Nigeria and internationally**: clarify what the business stands for, build an identity it can use consistently, then help apply it to a website or launch campaign where that is within the studio's confirmed delivery capacity. Nigeria is the studio's base, not the boundary of its intended market. This broad geography still needs a sharper initial audience and offer, validated through buyer conversations and qualified enquiries.

This is a proposed differentiation, not a proven market gap. Validate it through customer conversations and qualified enquiries. The sample includes many agencies claiming broad strategy, identity, digital, and campaign capabilities; simply listing those same services is unlikely to make the studio memorable. A narrower lead offer and clear application to the client's next business moment give the market a sharper reason to engage.

### Recommended presentation choices

- Lead with one clearly scoped Brand Foundation offer; treat website, campaign, and ongoing creative as next steps or add-ons, subject to founder-confirmed capability.
- Publish 2–3 well-explained genuine case studies as they become available. If there is no client work yet, show clearly labelled studio concepts and explain the brief, design choices, and intended application.
- Show the stages, client inputs, feedback points, and handoff. Make the process evidence of a good working relationship.
- Speak concretely to the two launch segments in the current strategy—businesses preparing to launch and established small businesses with an inconsistent brand—and learn which converts better.
- Keep the main CTA consistent and make the current human-operated enquiry path clear.
- Add proof only with permission. Label self-reported credentials and claims carefully; do not borrow competitor-style statistics or client logos.

### Differentiation questions to test with prospects

- What is the hardest part of getting a business ready to launch or refresh its brand?
- Which combination is most valuable first: brand clarity, identity, website, launch campaign, or ongoing support?
- What proof makes a small studio feel safe to hire: visible process, sample deliverables, references, founder access, or price clarity?
- Do prospects value Lagos-specific customer understanding, faster access to the creative lead, a fixed-scope package, or an integrated brand-to-website handoff enough to choose Cre8ive District?
