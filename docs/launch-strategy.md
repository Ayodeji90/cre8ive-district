# Cre8ive District — Startup Launch Strategy

> Working plan for the first public launch of the redesigned website and brand offer. Adapt dates, capacity, and spend once those are confirmed.

## Launch objective

Use the first 30 days to make Cre8ive District easy to understand, start qualified conversations, and learn which audience and offer responds. Treat this as a focused market test, not a promise of a particular revenue result.

**Primary outcome:** qualified project enquiries that lead to discovery conversations and written proposals.

**Secondary outcome:** evidence about which customer segment, problem, and message generates the strongest enquiries.

## Positioning to launch with

**Cre8ive District helps ambitious businesses build a distinctive brand, from clear strategy and visual identity to the creative assets that bring it into the world.**

Lead with branding. Present websites, campaigns, and ongoing creative as connected extensions of the brand offer. This makes the agency easier to remember than a broad list of unrelated services.

### Initial customer segments to test

Start with two segments and compare response rather than trying to speak to every business at once:

1. **New and early-stage businesses** preparing to launch or replace an improvised identity.
2. **Established small businesses** whose brand has become inconsistent as their products, audience, or ambitions have grown.

For the first month, qualify enquiries by business readiness, decision-maker access, scope, timeline, and budget fit. Do not treat every enquiry as a good-fit lead.

### Lead offer

Use a clear, scoped **Brand Foundation** offer as the first conversation anchor. Final deliverables and price should be set against the team's actual capacity and costs before promotion.

Possible scope to refine:

- Discovery and brand direction
- Positioning and core brand story
- Logo and visual identity system
- Short usage guide and agreed launch assets
- Handoff session and practical next steps

Offer a short discovery conversation as the low-friction next step. Avoid discounting before the team understands delivery time, margins, and the type of client it wants to attract.

## Launch funnel

| Stage | Customer action | Cre8ive District response |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Sees a post, referral, partnership mention, or search result | Show one clear problem, point of view, and example of the work |
| Consider | Visits the website or asks a question | Explain the process, scope, and fit in plain language |
| Enquire | Sends the website form or WhatsApp message | Acknowledge promptly and capture source, need, timing, and budget range |
| Qualify | Agrees to a discovery call | Confirm fit and decision-making process before preparing a proposal |
| Decide | Reviews a written scope and investment | Follow up against an agreed date; record outcome and reason |
| Refer | Completes a project and is satisfied | Ask for feedback and permission before sharing work or a testimonial |

The website form opens a prepared WhatsApp message. The person still has to press send, and the agency still has to record and follow up on the enquiry. Do not describe the form as an automated submission system.

## Channel plan

### Owned channels

- **Website:** Use the homepage to explain the brand offer and the contact page to start a conversation. Keep one primary CTA: “Start a project.”
- **Instagram:** Show identity details, process, before-and-after explanations when the underlying work and permission are real, and short founder-led explanations.
- **LinkedIn:** Share practical brand lessons, project decisions, launch reflections, and useful commentary for business owners and founders.
- **WhatsApp Business:** Keep the profile, business description, hours, contact number, and quick replies current. Use it to continue conversations people initiated; do not send unsolicited bulk messages.
- **Email:** Use a consistent agency address for proposals, project summaries, and follow-up.

### Relationship channels

- Tell past clients, colleagues, suppliers, and founder contacts what Cre8ive District now focuses on. Ask for relevant introductions rather than a generic share.
- Develop a small set of referral relationships with complementary providers such as web developers, printers, photographers, and business advisers. Agree how referrals are handled before making promises.
- Attend relevant founder and small-business events when there is a real opportunity to meet prospective clients. Track conversations and next steps instead of treating attendance as a result.

### Search presence

Check whether Cre8ive District qualifies for a Google Business Profile before creating one. Google requires eligible businesses to make in-person contact with customers during stated hours and does not allow online-only businesses; service-area businesses have specific address and service-area rules. If eligible, enter only accurate real-world business details and ask genuine clients for reviews. See [Google's eligibility guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036) and [service-area guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481).

### Paid promotion

Do not make paid ads the launch dependency. Begin with direct relationships and consistent owned content while the offer, response process, and proof are being validated. Consider a small paid test only when:

- The promoted offer and intended audience are specific.
- The landing path and WhatsApp number have been manually checked.
- Someone can respond to new enquiries and track outcomes.
- The test has a fixed spend ceiling the business can afford to lose.

If those conditions are met, test one audience and one offer at a time. Use actual qualified conversations and proposals as the decision signal, not reach or clicks alone. Do not scale until there is evidence that the agency can close and deliver the work profitably.

## Six-week rollout

### Preparation — 1 to 2 weeks before announcement

- Finalize the lead offer, deliverables, exclusions, timeline, revision terms, and price floor.
- Replace missing proof with real work samples. If using a self-initiated concept, label it clearly as a studio concept, not client work.
- Confirm the live website pages, WhatsApp number, email address, and mobile navigation manually.
- Prepare a one-page discovery brief, proposal template, invoice process, and lead tracker.
- Select one person responsible for first response and one backup.
- Prepare four weeks of useful content and a short launch announcement.
- Set a weekly delivery capacity limit so launch activity does not sell work the team cannot complete.

### Week 1 — Soft launch to warm contacts

- Share the site and positioning with a small set of relevant contacts.
- Ask for feedback on clarity: who the agency helps, what it offers, and what the next step is.
- Start individual conversations with people who can plausibly refer or need the service. Personalize each message; do not mass-message.
- Record questions, objections, and qualified enquiries in the lead tracker.

### Week 2 — Public launch

- Publish the launch announcement across the agency's active channels, adapting the copy to each audience.
- Publish a founder/studio introduction that explains why Cre8ive District is focused on branding.
- Share one useful breakdown of a brand decision or identity concept. Label concept work clearly.
- Ask referral partners for a specific introduction where there is a genuine fit.

### Week 3 — Demonstrate the process

- Publish a practical post about a common brand problem and how to diagnose it.
- Show the stages of a project from brief to identity system, using real work or clearly labelled studio concepts.
- Follow up with qualified enquiries and people who requested a proposal, on the date agreed with them.
- Review which message brought the best-fit conversations so far.

### Week 4 — Improve the offer and repeat what worked

- Publish answers to real questions heard during discovery conversations.
- Improve the website or proposal wording where prospective clients misunderstood the scope.
- Review lead sources, qualification, proposals, wins, losses, and delivery capacity.
- Keep the strongest message and offer; stop repeating content that attracts poor-fit enquiries.

### Weeks 5–6 — Focused second cycle

- Select the strongest segment and one secondary segment based on actual lead quality.
- Create a second content cycle around the strongest customer problem and offer.
- If paid promotion is appropriate, run one capped test and compare cost per qualified conversation, proposal, and client—not just cost per click.
- Set the next month's channel and delivery commitments from observed capacity and results.

## Content rhythm for the first month

Aim for a manageable cadence the team can sustain:

- **Two useful posts per week:** one insight or founder perspective, and one process or work breakdown.
- **One short video or story sequence per week:** answer a customer question or explain a brand decision.
- **One clear CTA per post:** invite a reply, a discovery conversation, or a visit to the project page.

Content themes:

1. Brand clarity: positioning, audience, differentiation, and common identity mistakes.
2. Craft: how type, colour, layout, and language support a brand idea.
3. Process: what a client can expect, what decisions they make, and how feedback works.
4. Proof: real project work, client feedback with permission, or clearly labelled studio concepts.

Avoid invented metrics, fabricated client names, false testimonials, fake scarcity, and unverified promises of sales or growth.

## Lead tracking and weekly review

Use a simple CRM board or spreadsheet with these fields:

- Date and source
- Contact and business
- Service requested
- Customer segment
- Budget range and timing
- Qualification status
- Next action and due date
- Proposal value and status
- Won/lost reason
- Permission to publish project work or feedback

Review these weekly:

- Enquiries by source
- Qualified enquiries
- Discovery conversations held
- Proposals sent and accepted
- Average response time
- Reasons for lost or unqualified opportunities
- Work sold versus available delivery capacity

Set numerical targets after confirming current capacity and the first week's baseline. For the first cycle, prioritize learning and clean tracking over vanity metrics.

## Launch-readiness checklist

- [ ] Brand Foundation scope and price floor approved
- [ ] At least three genuine work examples or labelled studio concepts ready
- [ ] Website copy, contact flow, email, and WhatsApp details checked
- [ ] Lead owner, backup, and response expectation agreed
- [ ] Discovery questions and proposal template ready
- [ ] Lead tracker created and weekly review booked
- [ ] Four weeks of content prepared
- [ ] Delivery capacity and project start dates understood
- [ ] Customer permission process in place for testimonials and published work
- [ ] Paid test budget, if any, has a hard cap and a clear success measure

## Decisions still to make

This plan intentionally does not set a launch date, price, or ad spend because the business's capacity, cost base, portfolio, and available budget have not been confirmed. Lock those decisions before publishing a time-limited offer or spending on paid promotion.
